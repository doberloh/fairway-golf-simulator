# Fairway 0.7 (beta)

Tag `v0.7`, published 2026-10-14.

Sound, your own bag of clubs, more believable greens, and a tidier screen while you play.

**Fairway makes sound now.** The club as you strike it — driver, woods, irons, chips and the putter each their own — the ball landing on every surface, clattering through trees, off rocks, the flagstick and houses, and dropping into the cup. A short tune for your score when you hole out. Every course has its own background: the wind, a bird now and then, crickets or cicadas when it is warm, a stream, creek, pond or the sea when you are near one. There is no club sound when you play with a launch monitor, since you will hear your own. A new **Sound** window, on the main menu and the in-round menu, turns it off or sets three volumes. Every sound is made by the game itself; there are no recordings.

**Your own bag.** Each golfer builds their own bag in **My bag**: up to 14 clubs, named however you like, from 19 kinds — the old eight plus 5 and 7 woods, 3 to 5 hybrids, 3, 4, 6 and 8 irons, and gap and lob wedges — each with the carry you actually hit it. The bag is kept in your profile and its backup, and the club list follows whoever is up. Your old club distances become everyone's starting bag.

**Greens behave more like greens.** A well-struck wedge no longer zips back a dozen yards: on most greens it checks and stops, and the hardest-spun shots come back a few yards at most, a little more on soft greens. The first bounce on a green is lower, about half what it was.

**Shot data, rearranged.** The **Last shot** line — whose shot, which club, Skip while the ball flies, and the arrow that hides or shows your numbers — now sits at the top of the shot controls, or in the launch monitor window when your monitor is armed. While the ball is in the air that window shrinks to just that line. The number tiles sit in a row along the bottom of the course, between the shot controls and the map. **On a phone**, three tiles run across the top of the screen — carry, total and backspin, unless you pick others in *Shot data*, which now has its own phone set.

**Launch monitor fix.** Choosing any club outside the original eight — a 4 iron, a hybrid, a gap wedge — used to make the server drop the game's connection. Every club in your bag now reaches your monitor.

**Smaller things.** Bushes and houses no longer stand in front of a tee and block your view down the hole. The flagstick is solid while it is in the hole, so a ball can hit it. Houses stop the ball where they are drawn (they were turned and too tall before). Flags stay visible from anywhere on the course. *Shot views* draws the ball's height as if the ground were flat, so its peak matches your apex, and the top-down view no longer shows a slice as a hook. In the course studio the button bar sits above any open panel instead of over it.

**Getting started**

1. Unzip the whole download into a normal folder.
2. Run **run_fairway_server** and keep its window open. The first time, Windows and macOS warn about a program from the internet: on Windows choose *More info → Run anyway*; on a Mac, Control-click it and choose *Open*.
3. Open `http://127.0.0.1:1922` in your browser. On a phone or tablet, open the second link the window prints.

No launch monitor? Just open **Fairway.html** from the download. `Fairway-source.zip` is the source code, and `RELEASE_SHA256.txt` lists each file's checksum.

Your players, saved rounds, courses and course codes from 0.6 carry over. Because bushes and houses moved out of the tees' view, a round saved in 0.6 opens with a note that its course has been rebuilt: carry on where you are, or start that course afresh. This is a beta: it hasn't been tried with every launch monitor or every device, and a weak graphics card may struggle even on the lowest setting.
