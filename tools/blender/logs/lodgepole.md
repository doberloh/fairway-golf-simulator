# lodgepole: iterations (tools/blender/cartoon_mountain.py)

One line a round: what was wrong beside the reference pictures, and what changed. The side-by-side of each round: bench/shots/blender/iter/cartoon_mountain/lodgepole-r<round>.png (local).

- r1: First build: a very thin bare trunk with a small crown in its top third. Beside the pictures it is a stick with a toy tree on top; real lodgepoles in a stand carry a narrow crown down 40-50% of the trunk, darker and fuller. 694 triangles. Next: crown from half height, a third wider, more tiers, the trunk a little thicker.
- r2: Crown down the upper half, a third wider, nine tiers: now a tall straight pine with a narrow crown, as the first picture's stand. The tiers are too regular a stack; real lodgepole crowns are tufty and uneven. Next: ragged tiers, a little more trunk.
- r3: Ragged tiers, a thicker trunk: a tall straight pine with an uneven narrow crown down its upper half; holds against the first and second pictures. Next: autumn and winter (evergreen), the far twin.
- r4: Held; checked the trunk's taper against the first picture's straight poles -- right. Next: the far twin.
- r5: Held; no change this round (the willow and lupine were the work).
- r6: Held this round; the crown and trunk read against the stand in the first picture. Next: spring, and its cost.
- r7: Spring: yellow-green new growth on the narrow crown, as the spec; 966 triangles, cheap. Next: autumn and the far twin.
- r8: Autumn (evergreen, unchanged) and the far twins: both twins keep the narrow crown down the upper half, 482 triangles. Next: winter.
- r9: Summer, final form: narrow and tall, the crown on its upper half, as the first two pictures. Next: winter, and its place in the game.
- r10: Winter (evergreen): unchanged, as the spec. The first picture shows what it still lacks: dead branch stubs all the way up the bare lower trunk of every pole. Next: the stubs.
- r11: Dead branch stubs up the bare lower trunk: the poles read like the first picture's stand now, the stubs small at this size but there. Next: hold; the far twin with the stubs.
- r12: Held; checked the stubs again at the sheet's size -- they read as roughness on the pole, as in the first picture. Next: the far twin.
- r13: The far twins with the stubs: the narrow crown and the pole survive, the stubs drop out at distance where they would not show anyway. Next: spring once more, then summer final.
- r14: Spring: yellow-green new growth, stubs on the pole; holds against the first two pictures. Next: summer, final.
- r15: Summer, final: a tall straight pole with dead stubs up its bare lower half and a narrow uneven crown above, as the first two pictures; 1,100 triangles, the far twin 480. Done.
