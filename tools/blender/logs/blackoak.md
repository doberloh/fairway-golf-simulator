# blackoak: iterations (tools/blender/cartoon_lakeshore.py)

One line a round: what was wrong beside the reference pictures, and what changed. The side-by-side of each round: bench/shots/blender/iter/cartoon_lakeshore/blackoak-r<round>.png (local).

- r1: Lineup (2,630 tris): a mushroom cap on a straight dark stick -- a fluffy ball. The pictures: crooked, leaning, open, a few dark masses on elbowed limbs. Next: a leaning bent trunk, three crooked limbs, three masses plus a top and a low side tuft, lopsided over the lean.
- r2: Crooked leaning trunk and elbowed limbs read as the dune oak; the masses are flat pads (an acacia umbrella) and BlackOak_1's side tuft sits on the trunk. Next: masses a fifth bigger and rounder, the tuft higher.
- r3: Rounder masses, lean and crooked limbs good, but the masses all sit at one height: a flat table, an acacia umbrella. Next: the three limbs end at stepped heights (0.52, 0.64, 0.76 H), the top mass higher, so the crown is irregular and taller.
- r4: Stepped heights fixed the table: crooked, leaning, lopsided, open. But each mass is a flat-bottomed pad, so BlackOak_2 reads as stacked bonsai pads. Next: round masses (no flat bottom, as tall as wide).
- r5: Round masses fixed the pads: a crooked leaning trunk, elbowed limbs, an open lopsided crown at stepped heights -- the dune black oak. Now it is three big balls; next: masses a little smaller, a fourth lobe on two of them for a ragged edge (2,200 tris, room to 2,630).
- r6: Ragged edge now -- crooked leaning trunk, elbowed limbs, an open lopsided crown of a few dark rounded masses at stepped heights; reads as the dune black oak and not as the inland oak's broad even crown. Holds.
- r7: Holds (unchanged this round): crooked, leaning, open, lopsided.
- r8: Autumn: russet-brown, the dark late oak colour; the crooked lopsided outline holds. Holds.
- r9: Far twins (shipped 790): the lean, the elbows and the lopsided masses hold. Holds.
- r10: Final, summer: a crooked leaning trunk, three elbowed limbs, an open lopsided crown of a few dark ragged masses at stepped heights -- the dune black oak, not the inland oak's broad even crown.
