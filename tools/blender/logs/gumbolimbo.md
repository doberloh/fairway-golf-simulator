# gumbolimbo: iterations (tools/blender/cartoon_keys.py)

One line a round: what was wrong beside the reference pictures, and what changed. The side-by-side of each round: bench/shots/blender/iter/cartoon_keys/gumbolimbo-r<round>.png (local).

- r1: r1: a single flared trunk under four pads -- a generic tree, too red. The pictures: a short trunk forking low into thick sinuous copper limbs that spread wide and fork again, a sparse open crown with the limbs showing. Next: short trunk, three thick limbs each forking twice, small flattish clumps on the tips with sky between.
- r2: Short trunk, three limbs forking twice: the copper limbs show, but the clumps are tiny pads -- a bonsai, not a spreading tree. Next: four limbs splayed wider and flatter, clumps twice the size on each tip, the bark less orange (salmon-copper as picture 2).
- r3: Four limbs, bigger clumps: now a cloud-pruned bonsai -- flat separate pads at different heights on copper limbs, a Japanese pine. The pictures: a broad open crown of loose rounded clumps over thick sinuous limbs. Next: clumps rounder and overlapping their neighbours, limbs thicker, sky kept only between limb groups.
- r4: Rounder clumps: still cloud-pruned pads on sticks -- a bonsai. The clumps sit only on the limb ends. Next: clumps of single big lobes along the outer half of each limb as well as its end, all bent to one crown centre, so they join into a broad open dome with gaps, the thick copper limbs bare below.
- r5: Lobes along the limbs: worse -- five big mushroom caps floating on copper sticks. Big single lobes, however placed, read as pads. Next: the madrone's way -- each of the eight limb ends forks again into two twigs, a small clump on each of the sixteen, so the crown is broken and airy and the thick limbs show through it.
- r6: Sixteen twig clumps: the limbs read -- thick, crooked, copper -- but the clumps are flattened pads in three layers, still a cloud-pruned bonsai. Next: round lobes, not flattened, at uneven heights, a second lobe beside every other twig, so the crown is one broken dome.
- r7: Spring flush (#7a9a4a) on round lobes: the copper limbs read and the crown is broken, not pads, but it sits low and squat -- the crown top at three quarters of the height, the limbs short and stubby, where pictures 1 and 3 are tall, the limbs long, the crown broad. Next: limbs reach higher (the crown up to the full 11 m), lobes a little bigger.
- r8: Taller: the crown now tops out near 11 m on long copper limbs -- closer to picture 3 -- but it is clumps on twig ends with sky all through, a Dr Seuss tree. Next: four lobes over the top between the limb groups, so the crown closes into a broken dome over bare limbs.
- r9: Top lobes added: the crown closes, but it is now eleven round green balls on copper sticks -- exactly the owner's 'fluffy balls'. Pictures 1 and 3: loose, fine, feathery leaf (pinnate) in rosettes at the twig ends. Next: every lobe becomes a flat ragged star (ct.skirt) -- a rosette -- on the twig ends, half-way along, and over the top.
- r10: Rosettes: no balls left -- thick crooked copper limbs carrying loose flat rosettes of leaf at uneven heights, sky between, the crown broad: a gumbo limbo's open, feathery crown (pictures 1, 3), and an outline nothing else in the pack has. A little shelf-like, like an acacia. Next: rosettes deeper and slightly bigger, so the crown reads as a broken dome. 4,830 triangles.
- r11: Deeper rosettes: the crown reads as a broken, feathery spread over thick copper limbs; still a little shelf-like, but nothing in the game has this outline and it is no ball. 4,830 triangles. Next: the far twin.
- r12: Far round: the twin keeps the copper limbs and rosettes, but at 2,830 of the full 4,830 it saves little -- the twigs cost. Next: the twin skips the twigs (each twig's kinks now drawn from a stream of its own, so skipping them moves nothing).
- r13: Winter (#55783a): holds -- copper limbs, rosette crown. Far twin without twigs: 2,150 triangles, from 2,830; the rosettes do not move.
- r14: Holds as copper limbs under a rosette crown, but the rosettes are level shelves of one size, a touch like the white pine's tiers. Next: sizes varied, a little more droop and raggedness, so the crown reads loose and feathery rather than shelved.
- r15: Summer, final: a short trunk forking low into four thick crooked copper limbs, forking twice more, carrying loose ragged rosettes of leaf at uneven heights, sky between -- the open feathery crown on red limbs of pictures 1 and 3, no balls. 4,830 triangles, far twin 2,150.
