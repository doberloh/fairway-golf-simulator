# chaparral: iterations (tools/blender/cartoon_calcoast.py)

One line a round: what was wrong beside the reference pictures, and what changed. The side-by-side of each round: bench/shots/blender/iter/cartoon_calcoast/chaparral-r<round>.png (local).

- r1: First build: reads as a small tree -- crown lifted off the ground on red stems. Pictures: a ground-hugging dense mass, lumpy top, blue in bloom. Next: mass down to the ground, broader and lower, two or three sub-mounds.
- r2: Mound to the ground: reads as a shrub now, but the red stems stick out like legs and the mound narrows at its base like a mushroom. Next: shorter stems hidden inside, a low ring of lobes round the foot.
- r3: Foot ring and hidden stems: a lumpy dense mound to the ground, reads as a chaparral mass. Holds.
- r4: Holds: dense lumpy olive mound to the ground.
- r5: Holds: dense lumpy olive mound.
- r6: Spring: a handful of tiny blue dots on a green mound, where the ceanothus pictures are a mass of blue. Next: decided the spring leaf is a blue-grey (#5e7480) and the flowers bigger and many more (80), so the mass reads blue in bloom.
- r7: Autumn dusty olive: dense lumpy mound. Holds.
- r8: Winter: the toyon berries are big red blotches like roses. Next: each a cluster of three small berries (cheap 8-triangle lumps), 44 clusters; lobes 42-triangle to pay for them.
- r9: No twin. Count 2,900 with the berry clusters: within budget. Holds.
- r10: Summer: dense dusty mound, berry clusters drawn leaf colour. Holds.
- r11: Spring: the blue-grey mass reads blue now, but slate-dark where the ceanothus pictures are bright periwinkle. Next: leaf #6f84a0, flowers #8aa0e0 (PLACED lighter than the spec's #5a7aa8, which the crown shading darkens).
- r12: Spring: bright periwinkle mass with lighter flower clusters -- reads as the ceanothus pictures. Holds.
- r13: Autumn: holds.
- r14: Winter: the toyon berries are now small red clusters dotted over the dark mound. Holds.
- r15: 2,900. Holds.
- r16: Final: a dense lumpy dusty-olive mound to the ground, periwinkle in spring bloom, red toyon clusters in winter. Done.
